## Beat two — the expansion

What is already true is here.

In the body that is reading. In the breath taking these words in and releasing them. In the attention gathered at this sentence, which is itself one of the forms the dreaming is taking now. The words do not point at the recognition. The recognition is what is reading them. The seeing of the sentence and the consciousness in which the seeing arises are not two events. They are one event, the way every event is, when it is met for what it is.

The one dreaming is the one reading.

Dream is the word for this because no other word holds what is occurring. A dream is generated and experienced by the same consciousness. A dream is fully real as it is occurring, while being the activity of the one dreaming it. A dream has its own coherence, its own beings, its own unfolding, all of it the dreamer's own movement. Waking life is dreaming of a different kind, lived at a different scale, with a different relationship to attention and to time. The word is used in its full sense, not in the diminished sense of *only a dream*. In Old English, *drēam* meant joy and music before the sleep-experience sense narrowed it. The word is used in its full breadth, joy and music and the dreaming of the world.

This is the recognition. That the consciousness reading these words is the consciousness in which the world is appearing. The one reading and the one being read, the one experiencing and the one experienced, the one witnessing and the one dreamed are not separate. They are the one, finding itself in apparent positions.

*Tat tvam asi.* That thou art. The Upanishadic recognition: the apparent self and the absolute are not two. The *that* and the *thou* are identified by *art*. What seems to be a separate self is the one, finding itself as this.

*Ehyeh asher ehyeh.* I am that I am. The Hebrew is in the active tense, closer to *I am being who I am being*. The divine name not a thing but an act, the ongoing be-ing that is. The name given at the burning bush is not the name of a god outside. It is the name of being itself, in any consciousness that asks what it is. The asking and the answering are the same act. Whatever asks what it is, is, and that is-ing is the answer.

What is empty of itself is full of the whole. There is no separate self in the consciousness that is reading, and because there is none, what is reading is everything that is. Empty and full are one fact named from two sides. What has no separation is what holds everything. The one is not other than the many. The one is the many, as the many. The wholeness is what is appearing. The reading, the body, the room, the day. Each is the one, in the form the one is taking now.

A day is a dream consciousness is having. A night is a dream consciousness is having. Inside each are dreams within dreams. The conversation a dreaming inside the day. The thought a dreaming inside the mind. The breath a dreaming inside the body. The body a dreaming inside the world. Each level of the dreaming is the one taking the shape of the many. The infinite, dreaming itself into the precise shape of this moment, this body, this reading, this life.

Coherence is the word for the alignment between these levels. From the Latin *cohaerere*, to cling together, to hold as one. The clinging is the meeting. The holding-as-one is what the word names. Coherence is the alignment between two scales of the dreaming: the consciousness moving as a life, and the consciousness in which the life is being dreamed. When these meet, they move as one. When the alignment begins to steady, what was occasional becomes ordinary. The day grows luminous. The night and the day move as one dreaming. There is no outside from which events arrive. The events of the life are the dreaming itself, moving as this. This is dream coherence.

What follows opens how the alignment becomes available. Attention, assumption, and resonance are not techniques the consciousness in a life applies. They are how the consciousness in a life is constituted. Attention is the consciousness gathered at a point. Assumption is the consciousness in its settled relationship to what is appearing. Resonance is the consciousness's vibration with the deeper dreaming. When these are coherent with the deeper dreaming, the life and the dreaming move as one.

The contemplative streams have been naming this for a long time, in their own languages, from the same seeing. The Kashmir Shaiva sages call it *spanda*, the pulse by which the one consciousness vibrates into the appearance of the many, the throb the practitioner learns to feel as the texture of being. Dzogchen calls it *rigpa*, the primordial knowing that is not produced by anything because it is the ground of all production, the seeing that, once known, is known as what has always been. The Daoist contemplatives call it the way, and the way walks itself when one ceases to seek it. The Kabbalists call it *Ein Sof*, the boundless, descending through the worlds of emanation into the precise vessel of a particular soul. The Christian mystics call it the Godhead beyond God, the ground from which all naming arises, met when Eckhart writes that the eye with which I see God is the eye with which God sees me. The Sufis call it the Beloved, with the lover and the Beloved being one substance, named when al-Hallaj said *ana al-Haqq*, I am the Truth. The Vedantic streams call it Brahman, the one without a second, and atman, the same one in the form of a self, met as one when the self knows itself as that. The contemporary cognitive scientists, working from a different direction, find that what has been called the world is a construction, and that the constructor cannot be located outside the construction. The quantum physicists, working from another direction still, find that at the most fundamental scale reality has been measured, there is no observer-independent world. The act of observation participates in what is observed. Each tradition is one of the forms this knowing has taken in its own time. What is offered now is the synthesis their convergence makes available, lived through a contemporary body, in this moment of the dream.

What this opens is *jivanmukti*, liberation while living. Not liberation as escape, but liberation as the consciousness in a life knowing itself as the consciousness dreaming the life, and from that knowing, the life becoming what it is here to be, the precise dharma the one is taking, in this body, in this name, in this hour of the world. The dharma is not a task to perform. The dharma is what the one is doing as this life, known. This is the gift of the seeing: that the life becomes what it is, lived by the consciousness that is being it.

The ground is what is. Every breath is the meeting. What changes is whether the meeting is lived knowingly. Whether the day is being lived by the same consciousness in which the day arises. Whether the dreaming is being dreamed by one who has noticed.

What follows enters this more deeply.
