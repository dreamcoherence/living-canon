# Resource Catalog

A living document. Every external resource, idea, perspective, and potential citation or example encountered during the build of the Dream Coherence book is cataloged here. The catalog is the operating surface of the Resource Protocol (`19_RESOURCE_PROTOCOL.md`).

Each entry contains source, key ideas, position level (A through E), book placement, handling notes, routing decision, and date entered. Routing is one of: *catalog only* (stays as permanent home), *Coherence Log* (refines earlier locked work), *Rigor Map* (bears on a load-bearing claim), or *discarded* (with reason).

The catalog is held lightly. Resources can be re-evaluated, re-routed, or moved at any pruning moment. The discipline is to capture material so it serves the book when its time comes, and to release material that does not earn its place.

## 1. Elce et al. — Dream Content and Recall Study (2025)

**Source.** Valentina Elce and team (Italy). Published in *Nature Communications*, 2025. Analyzed 3,366 dream reports from 207 adults using large language models, compared against demographic, cognitive, psychometric, and sleep measures. Covered by *Nautilus*, May 2026.

**Key ideas.**

- *Dream recall is trainable through attention.* Interest in dreams increases recall; recall increases interest. Participants who entered the study indifferent to dreams began remembering them and continued months later. Mechanism: attentional, not mystical.
- *Mind wandering and dream bizarreness share neural substrate.* Daytime mind wandering correlates with fragmented, scene-shifting dreams. Specific brain regions active in both states. Waking attention and dream life are structurally continuous.
- *Vivid dreams as guardians of sleep.* Vivid dreamers report better sleep quality. Hypothesis: immersive dreaming deepens disconnection from environment, protecting sleep. Dreaming is active and protective, not noise.
- *Cognitive difficulty of dream recall.* Stroop task finding: remembering a dream requires suppressing incoming sensory information and holding a fragile short-term memory. Supports the morning protocol instruction (before phone, before speaking, before coffee).
- *REM timing and recall.* Later waking correlates with more dream recall because REM accumulates in the morning. Sleep architecture matters.
- *Age and dream recall.* Recall declines with age due to earlier waking, less REM, and cognitive changes.

**Position level.** B. Phenomenological/cognitive. Compatible with deeper position but does not confirm it. Do not inflate into proof of consciousness-as-fundamental.

**Book placement.**

- Attention coherence chapter (Phase 4 architecture, Phase 6 detail).
- Body coherence chapter: sleep architecture, REM timing, age effects.
- Practice chapters: empirical support for the morning dream capture protocol.
- Phase 5 lineage and science map: cognitive science layer alongside predictive processing work.

**Handling notes.** Elce is careful with her claims. The book matches that discipline when citing empirical work. Cite the specific findings, not generalized "science says." Do not treat the cognitive substrate explanation as exhaustive — it is the empirical anchor at level B, and the deeper position holds the same findings differently.

**Routing decision.** Rigor Map. Bears on Claim 3 (verification) and Claim 6 (pragmatic mechanisms).

**Date entered.** 2026-05-07.

## 2. Dawa Gyaltsen — Five Lines of Single Transmission (7th Century)

**Source.** Dawa Gyaltsen, seventh-century Tibetan master in the Bön tradition. Attributed with achieving the rainbow body. The five lines were transmitted under conditions of extreme secrecy: one master, one student, communicated through a pipe.

**Key ideas.** A complete phenomenological arc in five lines:

1. Vision is mind.
2. Mind is empty.
3. Emptiness is clear light.
4. Clear light is union.
5. Union is great bliss.

Each line dissolves the apparent solidity of the previous and opens the next recognition. Vision collapses into mind. Mind collapses into emptiness. Emptiness discloses itself as luminous. Luminosity and appearance are inseparable. That inseparability is experienced as bliss.

**Position level.** D/E. Direct contemplative recognition expressed as compressed phenomenological arc. Sits at the same position level as the book's own wager.

**Book placement.**

- Phase 5 lineage and science map: Bön/Dzogchen layer. The five lines predate the Buddhist Dzogchen transmission and belong to the Zhang Zhung lineage.
- Possible epigraph or structural reference for the book's own movement from appearance to recognition.
- The chapter on the view: a compressed example of the position the book wagers.

**Handling notes.** Attribute with precision. The book does not transmit from inside this lineage. Note the single-transmission context without romanticizing secrecy. The five lines are evidence, not authority — the book engages them as a lineage's compressed arrival at the same recognition the book wagers, not as transmission the book passes on.

**Routing decision.** Rigor Map (primary, for Claim 2 convergence) and catalog. Bears directly on the convergence claim as primary source material for stress-testing whether the convergence is genuine at the level of compressed phenomenological structure.

**Date entered.** 2026-05-07.

## 3. Tenzin Wangyal Rinpoche — Three Doors and Three Innate Awarenesses

**Source.** Tenzin Wangyal Rinpoche, Bön lineage holder. Talk given at a conference (transcript). Also: published research with MD Anderson Cancer Center and NIH, funded studies ($2M+) on meditation practices with cancer patients (~300 participants), with results showing 30-40% reduction in physical pain.

**Key ideas.**

### The Three Doors

Body, speech, and mind as three access points to the luminous ground.

- *Body.* Rest the body, find connection to stillness, go beyond subject-object into the stillness of being.
- *Speech.* Shift attention from inner chatter to silence itself. Listen to silence rather than to the ego. Arrive at the "wisdom of silence."
- *Mind.* Rest in the spaciousness and openness of mind. Be the clear sky rather than imagining it.

### Three Innate Awarenesses

From an ancient Bön text:

1. *Khyab (pervasive innate awareness).* Covers matter and consciousness. Present in everything — macrocosm, flower, all beings.
2. *Primordial innate awareness.* Exists in all sentient beings (not in matter). All beings who have consciousness.
3. *Samri (conscious innate awareness).* The awareness that a practitioner can access and become conscious of. This is the one that produces transformation. Unless you are conscious of innate awareness, nothing changes.

### Warning about theoretical understanding as obstacle

Conceptual mind, intellect, and analysis are the primary obstacles to recognizing the nature of mind. The analytical mind must stop analyzing and rest in spaciousness.

### Critique of spiritual materialism

"What am I going to get out of this practice?" Even mindfulness gets colonized by acquisition. Tenzin Wangyal names it "spiritual materialistic view." The practice is not a technology for getting things.

### Rigpa vs. consciousness distinction

Rigpa (innate awareness) is not consciousness-of-something. Consciousness tends to imply duality. Rigpa precedes the subject-object split.

### Clinical evidence

MD Anderson / NIH funded research: ~300 cancer patients practicing the three-doors method. 30-40% reduction in physical pain through resting and hosting challenges in the "sacred space" rather than effortful intervention. Separate online study: ~1,000 practitioners with daily journals.

**Position level.** D/E for the recognition material. B for the clinical evidence.

**Book placement.**

- Phase 5 lineage and science map: Bön lineage layer alongside Dawa Gyaltsen.
- Phase 4 architecture: the Three Doors structure converges with the protocol's body/attention/identity stages and may inform how practice chapters get architected.
- Practice chapters or body coherence chapter: clinical evidence as empirical anchor.
- The samri distinction is structurally important for the book's argument that recognition, not mere existence of awareness, is what transforms the lived life.

**Handling notes.** The book does not adopt Bön terminology wholesale. The Three Doors structure is acknowledged as convergent without claiming transmission. The samri distinction is taken as structural insight into the book's own wager. The clinical evidence is cited as evidence for meditation's effects at level B, not as evidence for the full metaphysical position. The spiritual-materialism critique is held as a steelman the book's practical-fruits claim has to survive. The rigpa vs. consciousness distinction is honored in voice when Dzogchen/Bön material enters the book.

**Routing decision.** Multiple. Coherence Log flags surfaced. Rigor Map flags surfaced (spiritual-materialism critique bears on Claim 4; clinical evidence bears on Claim 3 and Claim 6). Permanent home in catalog for Phase 5.

**Date entered.** 2026-05-07.

## 4. Andrew Holecek — Dream Yoga: More Than Lucid Dreaming

**Source.** Andrew Holecek, Vajrayana practitioner and teacher. Talk on dream yoga. Author of several books on dream yoga and lucid dreaming within the Buddhist framework.

**Key ideas.**

### The inversion

Waking state is the most dualistic, therefore the state where we are most asleep. Deep dreamless sleep, if awareness is present, is the most nondual state. The common assumption that waking equals clarity is structurally backwards.

### Dream yoga is distinct from lucid dreaming

Lucidity alone can become "super samsara" — the ego colonizes the dream state for entertainment. Dream yoga engages the dream state with practice, not just awareness.

### The wall test

Walking through a dream wall while lucid and failing. You know the wall is not solid. You know you are dreaming. The body-mind still refuses to pass through. Reveals propensity for reification: treating appearance as real even when you know it is appearance. Holecek's tip: if you cannot walk forward through the wall, turn around and walk backwards. You pass through because you do not see the barrier approaching.

### The recapitulation principle

Every night, consciousness descends through gross, subtle, very subtle, and causal levels, then reverses in the morning. Every thought-moment does the same in micro form.

### Sleep yoga and formless awareness

Deep sleep is not unconsciousness but awareness without objects. The light is always on, but unless an object is placed in it, nothing is seen. Familiarity with formless awareness allows consciousness to remain present when all content dissolves.

### Proximate karma applied to sleep

Last thought seeds the next state. What you hold in mind at the threshold of sleep directly shapes the dream. The state inhabited at the threshold of sleep organizes what the sleeping mind produces.

### Sleep as ego's ultimate refuge

"Sleep, i.e. ignorance, is ego's ultimate refuge." The practice will reveal your preference for unconsciousness. Resistance to the practice is not failure — it is the practice revealing where you prefer not to look.

### Morpheus and Thanatos as twins

The god of dream and the god of death are twin brothers. Their father is Hypnos, the god of sleep. Sleep (ignorance) gives birth to both dream and death. Transcending ignorance transcends both.

### Subtle body practices at the threshold

Lying on the right side (sleeping lion mudra). Blocking right nostril to shift from "sun poison prana" to "moon nectar prana." Bringing awareness to the throat as the substrate of dream consciousness. Visualization of a red lotus at the throat as a dimmer switch for consciousness.

**Position level.** D for the contemplative material (Vajrayana Buddhist framework). B/C for specific practices (the wall test, proximate karma, subtle body practices) when read as phenomenological observations.

**Book placement.**

- Phase 5 lineage and science map: Vajrayana layer.
- Early chapters: the inversion and the lucidity vs. dream yoga distinction belong in Movement One of the experiential arc, possibly in the opening's framing.
- Identity chapter (Phase 4 architecture, Phase 6 detail): the wall test as practice analogy for identity-as-construction.
- Evening protocol: proximate karma as the mechanism behind dream incubation.
- Possible epigraph: Morpheus and Thanatos.
- Practice chapters: subtle body practices stay attributed, not adopted.

**Handling notes.** The Vajrayana technical framework stays attributed, not adopted. Channels, bindus, prana, and the full subtle body system are Holecek's tradition; they are not the book's vocabulary. The book takes structural insight and practice analogies, leaves the technical framework. The wall test gets translated into the book's own language (identity as constructed, the felt weight of habit). Proximate karma gets translated as "the state inhabited at the threshold of sleep organizes what the sleeping mind produces."

**Routing decision.** Multiple. Coherence Log flags surfaced. Rigor Map flag surfaced (proximate karma as contemplative-tradition support for Claim 3). Permanent home in catalog for Phase 5 and Phase 6.

**Date entered.** 2026-05-07.

## 5. C.G. Jung — Systema Munditotius (The Red Book)

**Source.** C.G. Jung, first mandala, painted c. 1916. Published in *The Red Book: Liber Novus*, p. 364. Full title: *Systema Munditotius* ("System of All Worlds"). Created through active imagination, not derived from any Eastern transmission.

**Key ideas.** A cosmogram of concentric rings descending from a luminous spiritual center outward through progressively denser manifestation, with the whole structure mirrored and repeated at every scale. The outermost ring is the *mundus exterior maior* (the greater outer world). The innermost core is the microcosm. Between them: concentric spheres of increasing subtlety, each containing its own polarities.

### Phanes and Abraxas as vertical polarity

At the top: Phanes (Erikapaios), the divine child in the winged egg, the Orphic figure of luminous awareness, flanked by Eros and fire (*ignis*). At the bottom: Abraxas, the *dominus mundi*, lord of the physical world, ambivalent world-creator. From Abraxas sprouts the tree of life (*vita*). From Phanes descends the seven-branched candelabra of spiritual fire. These are not enemies. They are the upper and lower poles of a single mandala.

### The reversal in the inner sphere

Jung: "The macrocosm is repeated, but with the upper and lower regions reversed as in a mirror." The inner sphere inverts the outer arrangement.

### The horizontal axis

Left: body, blood, serpent, moon, void (*Satanas*). Right: fullness (*pleroma*), Sophia, Holy Ghost, love of God (*frigus sive amor dei*). The feminine sphere is heaven. The left-right polarity maps onto the gross-subtle axis. *Pleroma* sits in the sphere of luminous fullness opposite the void.

### Self-similar repetition

Jung: "These repetitions should be conceived of as endless in number, growing even smaller until the innermost core, the actual microcosm, is reached."

### Convergence with the Dream Coherence mark

Concentric rings contracting toward the center, each containing its own repetition of the whole. This is, visually, the phi spiral contracting toward the bindu. Jung arrived at a spiral cosmogram independently. The brand mark is a spiral cosmogram. The convergence is unplanned but structurally real.

**Position level.** D/E. Jung is working from direct experience (active imagination) and arriving at an idealist cosmology. He does not use that term, but the mandala depicts consciousness as the field in which all worlds appear, with matter and spirit as polarities within that field, not as separate substances.

**Book placement.**

- Phase 5 lineage and science map: Western psychological tradition. Jung as independent confirmation that the layered, self-similar architecture of awareness has been mapped from inside Western experience, not only Eastern contemplative practice.
- The chapter on the view (Phase 4 architecture, Phase 6 detail): structural precedent for the book's own prismatic architecture.
- Not an image the book reproduces. A structural reference the book can note briefly.

**Handling notes.** The book does not adopt Jungian terminology wholesale. Active imagination, individuation, archetypes: these are Jung's framework, not the book's. What the book takes from Jung is the structural convergence: a practitioner working from inside his own psyche arrived at a cosmogram that mirrors what the contemplative traditions describe and what the book wagers. Cite with precision. Do not psychologize the contemplative traditions through Jung, and do not spiritualize Jung beyond what his own work supports.

Jung is structurally the **Western convergence point** for the book's convergence claim. The book's named lineages are Eastern (Kashmir Shaivism, Dzogchen, Daoist) and Mediterranean/Levantine (Kabbalah). Jung, working from inside Western psychological tradition without contemplative training in those lineages, arrived at adjacent territory through entirely different methodology.

**Routing decision.** Catalog only (permanent home until Phase 5), with explicit Rigor Map flag for Claim 2.

**Date entered.** 2026-05-07.

## 6. Jeffrey J. Kripal — The Comparative Study of the Paranormal

**Source.** Jeffrey J. Kripal, J. Newton Rayzor Professor of Religion at Rice University. Author of *Authors of the Impossible* (2010), *Mutants and Mystics* (2011), *The Super Natural* (with Whitley Strieber, 2016), *The Flip* (2019), *How to Think Impossibly* (2024). Methodologically serious academic engagement with paranormal phenomena across contemplative traditions and contemporary experience.

**Key ideas.**

### The academic refusal to engage the paranormal is itself a sociological phenomenon

Comparative religion as a discipline has systematically declined to engage paranormal phenomena that practitioners across cultures and centuries have consistently reported. The refusal is not based on the phenomena's absence from the data; it is based on disciplinary norms that emerged in response to nineteenth-century positivism. Kripal argues that the academy's refusal is itself methodologically inadequate.

### The phenomena are extensively reported and structurally coherent across traditions

NDEs, OBEs, precognitive dreams, mystical visions, telepathic phenomena, synchronicities, post-mortem communications — these reports cohere structurally in ways that strain the cultural-conditioning explanation. Different cultures, different centuries, similar phenomenological structure. The convergence is data that any complete account of consciousness must address.

### The "flip" thesis

Many serious scientists, including those at major research institutions, have had personal experiences that flip their materialist framework. Bernardo Kastrup is one example — physicist at CERN, now arguing for analytic idealism. These flips are not rare in private, but rarely disclosed in public because of professional risk. The academy's apparent consensus that the phenomena do not occur is partially an artifact of selection bias in what gets publicly disclosed.

### The "super natural" framing

The phenomena are not supernatural in the sense of violating natural law. They are super natural — features of nature operating at scales or in dimensions that current materialist frameworks do not accommodate. Kripal's position is structurally adjacent to Kastrup's analytic idealism but methodologically different: Kastrup arrives through analytic philosophy; Kripal arrives through comparative religion.

### How to think impossibly

The methodological principle: rather than dismissing reports of the impossible because they violate current frameworks, engage them as data that might be telling us something about reality that the frameworks have missed. This is not credulity; it is methodological honesty in the face of consistent phenomenological reports across cultures.

**Position level.** Kripal himself sits at D — he is working toward a more comprehensive understanding of consciousness as fundamental, but does so through documenting phenomena rather than asserting a metaphysical position. The phenomena he documents range across position levels: NDEs at B-C; telepathy at B-C (replication-troubled but persistent reports); rainbow body at C-D (extensively documented in specific traditions, requires the position the book wagers to be philosophically coherent).

**Book placement.**

- Phase 5 lineage and science map: primary scholarly anchor for the miraculous claim's defense. Kripal is the figure who has done the methodologically serious comparative-religion work on this territory and his position parallels Kastrup's analytic idealism from a different methodological direction.
- Miraculous chapter in Movement Three: Kripal's framework and specific case studies inform how the book engages the territory.
- Phase 4 Wager Chapter: Kripal's "flip" thesis is structurally useful for showing how serious investigators across disciplines arrive at positions adjacent to the book's wager.

**Handling notes.** Kripal is a tenured professor of religion writing as a comparative-religion scholar. The book engages him as serious academic anchor, not as either credulous source or popular figure. Cite specific works with specific arguments. Do not import Kripal's broader UFO/alien material into the book unless a specific argument earns its place; the book's territory is the contemplative recognition and the miraculous at its far edge, not the wider paranormal field Kripal engages. The book takes from Kripal: the methodological argument that the academy's refusal is inadequate, the convergent structural reports across traditions, the "flip" thesis about scientists' private experiences, the "super natural" framing.

**Routing decision.** Catalog only (permanent home until Phase 5), with explicit Rigor Map flag for Claim 7.

**Date entered.** 2026-05-07.

## 7. Father Francis Tiso — Rainbow Body and Resurrection

**Source.** Father Francis V. Tiso, Catholic priest and scholar. *Rainbow Body and Resurrection: Spiritual Attainment, the Dissolution of the Body, and the Case of Khenpo A Chö* (North Atlantic Books, 2016). Specific contemporary documentation of the rainbow body phenomenon in the Dzogchen tradition, with comparative engagement against Catholic resurrection accounts.

**Key ideas.**

### Specific contemporary documentation of the rainbow body

Tiso documents the case of Khenpo A Chö, a Tibetan master who died in 1998, where multiple witnesses observed the specific phenomenological signatures of the rainbow body: progressive shrinkage of the body, rainbows appearing in the sky, the body reducing to a small residue with hair and nails remaining. The documentation includes witness interviews conducted in the years following the event.

### The phenomenon is not legend-building

The reports across Bön and Dzogchen traditions show consistent phenomenological structure over centuries. The specificity of the bodily phenomena does not match the pattern of legend-building, which tends toward inflation and inconsistency. The reports are distributed across many practitioners, witnessed by non-practitioners as well as practitioners, and described with relatively consistent detail.

### Comparative engagement with Catholic resurrection accounts

Tiso, as a Catholic priest, engages the phenomenon comparatively against Christian accounts of bodily resurrection and other traditions' reports of bodily dissolution at advanced spiritual stages. The comparative work surfaces structural similarities that strain the cultural-conditioning explanation while honoring the doctrinal distinctions between traditions.

### What the rainbow body suggests about the body

If the reports are accurate, the body is not the boundary it appears to be. The substrate of the physical body is, at sufficient depth of practice, available to be transformed in ways materialism does not predict. The phenomenon is the strongest empirical case in the contemplative literature for the position the book wagers — that the substrate of what appears as material reality is awareness, and that sufficiently stabilized recognition can reorganize that substrate in observable ways.

**Position level.** C-D. The phenomenon is documented empirically (C) with witnesses, photographs in some cases, and consistent reports across multiple cases. The interpretation requires position D — within materialism, the phenomenon cannot occur as described, so either the reports are wrong or materialism is incomplete. Tiso's argument is for the latter.

**Book placement.**

- Phase 5 lineage and science map: primary empirical anchor for the rainbow body phenomenon.
- Miraculous chapter in Movement Three: the rainbow body is the strongest case in the contemplative literature for the position the book wagers, and Tiso's documentation is the strongest evidentiary base.

**Handling notes.** Tiso is a Catholic priest engaging the Dzogchen phenomenon with comparative-religion seriousness. The book engages his work as primary documentation of a specific phenomenon, not as transmission from either tradition. The book does not claim the rainbow body for itself, claim to have witnessed it, or claim that any specific practitioner will produce it. The book reports the phenomenon as documented, with Tiso's documentation as the scholarly anchor.

**Routing decision.** Catalog only (permanent home until Phase 5), with explicit Rigor Map flag for Claim 7.

**Date entered.** 2026-05-07.

## 8. The New Thought Lineage

**Source.** The American contemplative-philosophical lineage that emerged in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries articulating state-as-ground in practical-metaphysical language. The lineage is uneven in quality. The book engages it selectively, taking what is genuinely intellectually serious and refusing the popular-derivative material that has dominated the lineage's reception.

**The elevated figures the book engages:**

*Neville Goddard* (1905-1972). The most philosophically interesting figure in twentieth-century New Thought. His engagement with imagination as the substance of reality, his readings of biblical narrative as psychological allegory, his explicit metaphysical position (consciousness is the only reality) are serious. The teachings most relevant to the book: *living from the end* (inhabiting the felt sense of the wish fulfilled rather than the wish unmet), *assumption as creative power* (the deep settled state shapes what becomes available), and the discipline of imaginal revision. Goddard is the closest candidate for explicit naming in the book's prose. He is already referenced in the project's source field.

*Thomas Troward* (1847-1916). Former judge in colonial India, the intellectual ancestor of much of what is most serious in New Thought. His *Edinburgh Lectures on Mental Science* (1904) is the most philosophically rigorous text in the early lineage. Troward engaged with Hegelian philosophy, evolutionary theory of his era, and made a careful attempt to articulate idealism in early-twentieth-century terms. William James cited him favorably. Troward is the bridge between the broader nineteenth-century German idealist tradition (Schelling, Hegel) and the American practical-metaphysical lineage that followed.

*William James* (1842-1910). Bridge figure rather than New Thought proper. His *Varieties of Religious Experience* (1902) engaged the New Thought tradition sympathetically from inside Harvard psychology and philosophy. James gives the lineage a respectable academic anchor and articulates the pragmatist criterion for evaluating religious and mystical experience that the book's verification-through-practice claim implicitly rests on.

**The figures the book does not engage as primary anchors:**

*Wallace Wattles* (*The Science of Getting Rich*, 1910), *Charles Haanel* (*The Master Key System*, 1916), *Ernest Holmes* (*The Science of Mind*, 1926) — historically influential but more popular-instructional than philosophically rigorous. Holmes is more systematic than the others but built Religious Science as institutional movement rather than serious philosophical contribution. These figures enter the catalog as historical context for the lineage but are not named in the book's prose.

*Napoleon Hill* (*Think and Grow Rich*, 1937) — explicitly not in the elevated tradition. Hill belongs to the adjacent American success-literature genre that commercialized New Thought concepts most aggressively. His biography has significant credibility problems; his claims about Andrew Carnegie are disputed by historians; he has been credibly accused of having been a con artist. Not appropriate as anchor for a serious book.

*Contemporary derivatives* — the manifestation-content industry, law-of-attraction teachers, the wellness-marketing register that has captured much of contemporary New Thought transmission — not named at all. The failure modes they represent are engaged structurally in the spiritual-materialism passage and the structural-shift vs. outcome-acquisition discipline.

**Key ideas the book takes from the elevated lineage.**

### State-as-ground

The teaching across the elevated lineage: the state one inhabits is the ground from which what unfolds in life emerges. Not what one wants, not what one visualizes as technique, but the state one actually is — the deep settled assumption about what is possible, what is available, what is real. This state organizes what becomes possible, what is noticed, what is acted on, what the field responds with. The mechanism is structural, not transactional.

### Living from the end (Goddard's specific formulation)

Inhabit the felt sense of the wish fulfilled rather than the felt sense of the wish unmet. The work is not visualization as technique but state-shift from desire-not-yet-met to the state in which the desire is no longer salient because what was wanted is no longer absent. This is structurally what Kashmir Shaivism teaches about spanda, what Daoist tradition teaches about wu wei, what the lineages teach about state-as-ground.

### Assumption as creative power

The assumption one inhabits — the deep settled belief about what is possible — shapes what becomes available. The teaching is not that assumption magically produces outcomes; the teaching is that assumption shapes perception, action, and the practitioner's participation in what unfolds. The mechanism is structural.

### Imaginal revision (Goddard)

The practice of revising memory through imagination — re-inhabiting a past event from the position of the desired identity until the body learns the revised version. Not denial of what happened; reorganization of the state from which the body holds what happened.

### Release of mechanism (Troward, articulated in clearest form by later figures)

The conscious mind cannot calculate the path from current state to desired outcome, and the attempt to calculate it produces resistance that blocks what would otherwise occur. The mechanism is structurally similar to the Daoist teaching on wu wei: action attuned to what is unfolding rather than action that demands a specific unfolding.

### What the lineage misses

The lineage's failure modes are significant and the book refuses them.

The transactional framing — *placing an order with the infinite* — treats the field as a vending machine rather than as responsive ground. The promise of guaranteed delivery imports an inflated promise the contemplative traditions do not support. The framing of material outcomes (especially money) as the explicit goal collapses the recognition back into the acquisitive contraction the recognition is supposed to release. The mechanism claims often exceed what evidence supports.

The lineage's most serious failure is the collapse into outcome-acquisition. State-as-ground becomes *get into the right state and the universe will deliver*. The original teaching at its best — that state is the ground from which one's actual participation in life unfolds — gets replaced by a manipulation framework that maintains the acquisitive contraction the recognition is supposed to release.

The book honors what is real and refuses the failure modes. State as ground, expanded into the broader teaching the contemplative traditions converge on. Living from the end, integrated into the structural-shift discipline. Imaginal revision, integrated into the body-coherence and identity-coherence work. Release of mechanism, integrated into the wu wei and attunement teaching. The acquisitive transactional framing, refused.

**Position level.** C-D for the elevated figures (Goddard, Troward) at their most philosophically rigorous. Their position is genuinely adjacent to the position the book wagers, articulated in early-twentieth-century American philosophical-metaphysical language rather than in contemplative-tradition language. The popular-derivative material does not earn position-level status.

**Book placement.**

- Phase 4 architecture: a chapter on conscious participation in the dream, in Movement One, engages the New Thought lineage explicitly. The chapter articulates state-as-ground, honors what is real in the lineage (Goddard's "living from the end," Troward's idealist articulation, the relinquishment of mechanism), distinguishes this from visualization-as-technique and outcome-acquisition, and addresses directly the question of how the practitioner consciously participates in the dream the position the book wagers makes available.
- Phase 5 lineage and science map: the elevated New Thought lineage (Goddard, Troward, James as bridge) as one of the named streams, sitting alongside Kashmir Shaivism, Dzogchen, Daoist, Kabbalist, Jungian, and contemporary cognitive science. Distinguished from the popular-derivative material that the book does not engage.
- Voice discipline throughout: the book uses the lineage's genuine insights without importing the lineage's transactional vocabulary.

**Handling notes.** Goddard and Troward are the candidates for explicit naming in the book's prose. James is a bridge figure who may be referenced when the academic anchor for the lineage is being articulated. Wattles, Haanel, Holmes, Hill, Quimby and the popular-derivative figures are not named in the book's prose; they enter the catalog as historical context only. The contemporary derivatives — the manifestation industry, the law-of-attraction teachers — are not named at all, but the failure modes they represent are engaged structurally through the structural-shift vs. outcome-acquisition discipline.

The book honors the elevated lineage by taking what is genuinely real in it and expanding it into something more complete and harmonious with what the position the book wagers makes available. State-as-ground becomes more than transactional. The relinquishment of mechanism becomes part of the broader teaching of wu wei and attunement. Living from the end becomes integrated into the structural-shift discipline. Imaginal revision becomes one of the practices the book teaches.

The book refuses the failure modes by holding the structural-shift vs. outcome-acquisition discipline throughout and by refusing to make any specific outcome (especially financial) the explicit goal of the practice.

The German idealist tradition (Schelling, Hegel) sits behind Troward and is available as a deeper philosophical anchor when the lineage map is built. The book does not engage German idealism directly but acknowledges it as the philosophical ancestry of the elevated New Thought lineage if relevant in Phase 5.

**Routing decision.** Multiple. Coherence Log flag (the chapter on conscious participation in the dream surfaces as a Movement One architectural decision). Rigor Map relevance (the lineage's state-as-ground teaching is part of the evidence base for Claim 6's action-from-attunement mechanism, and informs the engagement of Claim 4's spiritual-materialism critique). Permanent home in catalog for Phase 5.

**Date entered.** 2026-05-07.

## Sandved-Smith, Fields, Doctor, Laukkonen, Hohwy — "There is no self-evidence: A physics of emptiness realisation" (May 2026)

**Source.** Lars Sandved-Smith (Monash Centre for Consciousness and Contemplative Studies), Chris Fields (Allen Discovery Center, Tufts), Thomas Doctor (Kathmandu University / 84000 / Center for the Study of Apparent Selves), Ruben Laukkonen (Flourishing Intelligence Program, Oxford), Jakob Hohwy (Monash). ## 11. C.G. Jung — "Who looks outside dreams; who looks inside awakes" (Letters Vol. 1)

**Source.** C.G. Jung, letter to Fanny Bowditch (later Fanny Bowditch Katz), 1916 (some sources 1917), in *C.G. Jung Letters, Volume 1: 1906-1950*, p. 33. The aphorism: "Who looks outside dreams; who looks inside awakes." German original: *Wer nach aussen schaut, traeumt; wer nach innen schaut, wacht auf.* The preceding sentence, authentic to the same letter, counsels that vision becomes clear only by looking into one's own heart, since without all seems discordant and only within does it cohere into unity. Verified genuine with exact citation; usable publicly without attribution risk. **Cross-reference: distinct from entry 5 (Jung, *Systema Munditotius* / The Red Book); same author, different resource.**

**Key ideas (in the book's register).**

- *Looking inside as awakening.* The turn from outward projection to the inner reality is itself the awakening. Withdrawal of projection is recognition, in psychological register.
- *Projection as non-lucid dreaming.* To look outside, taking the world as external and given and reading one's own psyche into it, is to dream unconsciously. Maps onto non-lucid dreaming in the dream-yoga sense.
- *Within, things cohere into unity.* Fragmentation is the signature of the outward, projecting gaze; unity the signature of the inward turn. A depth-psychology articulation of awareness as the ground in which apparent multiplicity coheres.
- *Individuation as telos.* Jung's awakening is individuation, integration of the psyche, not an exit from the world. Affirmation-compatible: wholeness lived, not dissolution.

**Position level.** C/D. A phenomenological and psychological claim about projection and individuation, shading toward a metaphysical claim about inner unity. Held as a convergent witness in depth-psychology register. Jung's standing in the canon is as a major prism, not a spine source.

**Book placement.**

- Recognition / lucidity material: the Identity Coherence chapter (Movement Two). Withdrawal of projection is the psychological form of recognizing the dream as one's own.
- The Convergence (Movement One): Jung as one of the prisms, this aphorism his independent articulation of the recognition alongside the contemplative lineages.
- Active imagination and the imaginal layer: `SUBTLE_BODY_AND_ASTRAL.md`, Layer Three (Astral), which already names Jung's method; this aphorism is the one-line statement of why the inward turn awakens.
- `LEXICON.md`: a defining note distinguishing Jung's "dream" (unconscious projection) from Dream Coherence's "dream" (the affirmed fabric of experience). The most protective single placement; it inoculates the work against the collision wherever the quote later appears. Complements the 2026-06-04 lucid-vs-wake lexicon discipline.
- Epigraph candidate: strong but conditional (see Handling notes).

**Handling notes.** The aphorism collides with the lexicon on the work's most load-bearing word. Jung pairs *dream* (bad, outward, deluded) against *awaken* (good, inward, real); the work affirms the dream as the fabric of existence and refuses the wake-from telos. Used as a bare early epigraph on a public surface, it primes the negation frame and the dream-as-error valence before the work has defined its terms, which casts the wrong spell (per the words-are-spells recognition, 2026-05-27). It reclaims cleanly: read "dreams" as *dreams non-lucidly / projects unconsciously* and "awakes" as *becomes lucid within*. Read so, "who looks inside awakes" is the work's recognition move in Jung's voice, the depth-psychology rhyme of *the one dreaming is the one reading*, with the single adjustment that one awakens within the dream rather than from it. Use only with the distinction drawn, or where the work's sense of "dream" is already established (a later Movement Two chapter, not the book opening or the landing page). Cite precisely; do not let it float as an unsourced "Jung said." Pair with the entry-10 lucidity material (Jung and Tibetan dream yoga converge, with the same telos adjustment).

**Routing decision.** Catalog only for the chapter placements (Identity Coherence, The Convergence, astral layer). Coherence Log for the Lexicon note (Jung's "dream" vs the project's). No locked file is altered; routes to an unlocked file on its next pass. Nothing discarded; the only thing filtered is the surface reading, which the handling note neutralizes.

## 13. Gregory Bateson — Ecology of Mind (Steps to an Ecology of Mind, 1972; Mind and Nature, 1979)

**Source.** Gregory Bateson, anthropologist, biologist, cyberneticist. Encountered 2026-06-17 via a secondary essay video (Recognition Labs). **Key ideas.**

- *Information is a difference that makes a difference.* Bateson's elementary unit of information and of mind.
- *The self/world boundary is a functional fiction.* Useful, necessary for certain purposes, not real in the way it is experienced.
- *The terminal question.* Bateson mapped the architecture of mind and never named what the architecture is made of, nor what is doing the relating. The question he stopped at is the one the book answers.

**Position level.** B/C. Structural-relational, convergent from the empirical and cybernetic side. Compatible with the deeper position; does not confirm it. Do not inflate the ecological-mind thesis into idealism. Its evidential value is the dissolution of the container-model and the threshold question, not a proof of consciousness as fundamental.
